Shoulder rules — every session

Crossover Symmetry Activation before you lift. Recovery after. Also on both off days. Six minutes a week decides whether you're still training in month six.

Nothing loaded overhead for 12 weeks. No barbell press, no bench, no dips, no upright rows, no wall balls or slam balls, no kipping. They come back when a clinician clears you.

You have run this exact block five times. Every one has the same shape: start light, add 10 lb a session, climb for seven or eight weeks, peak somewhere in the 230s, stall, spiral down for three weeks, stop. The last one peaked at 235 on March 9th and you were back to 200 by April 19th. Then twelve sessions in February became eight in March and three in April — the log shows you drifting out a full month before you actually quit.

Nothing was wrong with your effort. Linear progression just ends, and Starting Strength has no next chapter for a 48-year-old who has already been there five times. That is what the deloads in weeks 4, 8 and 12 are for, and why nothing here goes above 78%. The plan is built to be boring in week 3 so that week 11 still happens.

The press is the tell. Overhead press: 140 lb in 2022, 140 in 2023, capped at 105 across all of 2026 — a 25% regression while your squat gave up only 4%. Nothing else in the log fell like that. Whatever the shoulder is doing, it has been doing it for a while, and it is the reason there is no barbell press in this program.

The good news is in the same data. January to March 2026 you took the squat from 95 to 235 in seven weeks. That is not a beginner's curve, that is muscle you still have coming back online. Four months off has cost you less than it feels like.

Progression: main lifts +5 lb lower / +2.5–5 lb upper per week, only if every rep was clean. Accessories use double progression — hit the top rep target on all sets, then add weight. Weeks 4, 8 and 12 are deloads: same movements, half the reps, 60% of the weight.
